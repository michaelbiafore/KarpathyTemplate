# Summary

Summary
In this chapter, we explored the intricacies of going from a single-agent system to
a MAS. The complexity of such systems starts from the orchestration of agents. We
covered common patterns (centralized, decentralized, hierarchical, and federated)
for orchestrating agents and common frameworks for building them (CAMEL, Met‐
aGPT, AutoGen, LangGraph, and CrewAI). In orchestration, communication is of
the utmost importance, so we covered the multi-agent equivalent of MCP, namely
Agent2Agent (A2A).
As agents start to collaborate, we can look toward MASs as agent societies. How
agents communicate and interact with each other provides valuable insight into their
AI psychology. We covered how LLMs and agents can mimic emotional intelligence
to a certain degree by exploring their Theory of Mind capabilities as well as their
social identities. We explored a famous experiment where agents were put in a
pixel-sandbox environment and planned their days, went to group activities, and
formed relationships. The insights that we can get from AI psychology allow for an
understanding of how agents may interact not only with each other, but also with
humans, and where they might fail.
We ended this chapter with a brief look into a common use case for MAS, namely
Deep Research, which allows for extensive research through agents on a given topic.
We covered the Agent Laboratory and the AI co-scientist as examples of how the
orchestration of such a system is implemented.
In the next chapter, we’ll explore how LLMs, as the backbone of agents, can process
modalities other than text.
