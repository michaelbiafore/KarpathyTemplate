# Afterword

Afterword
You bought this book. That was the last transaction in it.
Everything else inside arrived as a gift. The methods, habits of mind, small tricks
that keep an agent on task: we invented almost none of them. They came to us
hand-to-hand, from people who worked something out and then, instead of keeping
it, wrote it down and let it go. Most of them we never paid. Most we could not have.
The botanist Robin Wall Kimmerer draws the distinction we want to leave you with.
A transaction is even: you pay, you receive, the books close, and you walk away owing
no one. A gift is not even, and it never closes. It asks one thing of whomever receives
it: that it keeps moving. Held still, hoarded or buried, it stops being a gift and begins
to rot.
This is not an exchange we are closing. It’s a gift we’re handing on, and it travels with
you now, out to people and places you’ll never see. You answer for what it does there.
So we hope you build well. We hope what you make outlasts this book and our
names, reaches someone we’ll never meet, and is good to them. We hope you become
one of the people the rest of us are quietly counting on.
And then, the way a gift does, it keeps going.
